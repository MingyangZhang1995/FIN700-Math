Regression Discontinuity

The design in one sentence

A rule changes treatment at a known threshold. RD compares observations just below and just above that threshold.

Suppose students with an entrance score of at least 80 are eligible for tutoring. Compare a student who scored 79.9 with one who scored 80.1. Their scores are nearly identical; their eligibility differs.

The design asks:

Does the outcome jump at the same place where the treatment rule jumps?

Vocabulary before estimation

Let \(R_i\) be the original entrance score and let the cutoff be \(c=80\). Define the centered score

\[ X_i=R_i-c. \]

The cutoff is now zero. Negative values are below it; positive values are above it.

Term Symbol in these notes Tutoring example
Running variable (assignment or forcing variable) \(R_i\) or centered \(X_i\) Entrance score
Cutoff (threshold) \(c\) 80 points
Eligibility indicator (assignment or encouragement) \(Z_i=\mathbb 1(X_i\geq0)\) in this example Eligible for tutoring
Treatment received \(D_i\) Attended tutoring
Outcome \(Y_i\) Later exam score
Bandwidth \(h\) Use scores from \(80-h\) to \(80+h\)
Discontinuity (jump) Difference of limits at \(0\) Right-hand limit minus left-hand limit
RD estimand \(\tau_{RD}\) Effect at the eligibility margin
ImportantThree statements that prevent most notation mistakes
  1. The running variable determines cutoff side; the rule maps cutoff side into eligibility.
  2. In a sharp RD, cutoff side and treatment are the same. In a fuzzy RD, they are not.
  3. The RD effect is a vertical jump at the cutoff, not the slope of the outcome against the running variable.

Design What happens at the cutoff? What \(Z_i\) means
Sharp RD \(D_i\) changes from 0 to 1 Eligibility; it also equals treatment status

“Sharp” does not mean the outcome jump is large. “Fuzzy” does not mean the cutoff is unknown.

Why the comparison can be causal

Students with scores of 60 and 90 may differ in many ways. Students with 79.9 and 80.1 have nearly the same running variable. The cutoff supplies a reason that their treatment status differs sharply.

The key counterfactual claim is continuity:

Without the treatment rule, average potential outcomes would pass smoothly through the cutoff.

Levels and slopes may vary with the running variable. They just must not jump at the cutoff for another reason.

  const x = Array.from({length: 81}, (_, i) => -10 + i * 0.25);

At the cutoff, the observed jump is

\[ \tau_{RD} = \lim_{x\downarrow 0}E[Y_i\mid X_i=x] - \lim_{x\uparrow 0}E[Y_i\mid X_i=x]. \]

Here \(x\downarrow0\) approaches zero from above and \(x\uparrow0\) approaches from below. The order is right minus left.

Under continuity, this identifies the average treatment effect at the cutoff:

\[ \tau_{RD}=E[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\mid X_i=0]. \]

This is local. It does not automatically reveal the effect for students far above or below 80.

Three jumps, three names

This table connects RD to IV terminology.

Plot at the cutoff Jump Common name
Eligibility \(Z\) against \(X\) \(1\) by construction Assignment rule
Treatment \(D\) against \(X\) \(\Delta_D\) First stage
Outcome \(Y\) against \(X\) \(\Delta_Y\) Reduced form

For a sharp RD, \(\Delta_D=1\), so the treatment effect is simply \(\Delta_Y\).

For a fuzzy RD,

\[ \boxed{\tau_{FRD}=\frac{\Delta_Y}{\Delta_D}}. \]

Example: eligibility raises tutoring participation by 40 percentage points and raises later scores by 2 points. The fuzzy RD estimate is

\[ \frac{2}{0.40}=5 \]

score points for students whose tutoring decision is changed by eligibility near the cutoff. This is a local average treatment effect for compliers at the cutoff.

From the picture to an estimate

Center first

Centering makes the cutoff \(X_i=0\). Then the intercept on each side is the predicted outcome at the cutoff.

Fit each side separately and locally

Within a bandwidth \(h\), a local linear specification is

\[ Y_i =\alpha+\tau Z_i+\beta X_i+\gamma(Z_i\times X_i)+\varepsilon_i, \qquad |X_i|\leq h. \]

Quantity Left of cutoff Right of cutoff
Intercept at \(X=0\) \(\alpha\) \(\alpha+\tau\)
Slope \(\beta\) \(\beta+\gamma\)

Therefore \(\tau\) is the estimated vertical jump. The interaction allows the two sides to have different slopes.

TipWhy “local linear”?

Near a point, many smooth curves look approximately straight. Fit simple lines near the cutoff rather than a complicated curve across the full range.

Bandwidth is the central tradeoff

Bandwidth Main advantage Main risk
Narrow Better local approximation; units are more comparable Few observations; imprecise estimate
Wide More observations; greater precision Curvature can bias a simple local fit

Use a data-driven bandwidth as the main specification and show nearby alternatives as sensitivity checks. Do not choose \(h\) because it makes the estimate significant.

Bins are for the picture

An RD plot often shows means within bins of the running variable. Bins reduce visual noise; they usually do not define the estimator.

Choice Controls Affects
Bin width / number of bins How raw outcomes are summarized Appearance of the plot
Bandwidth \(h\) Which observations enter local estimation Estimate and precision
Polynomial order Shape fitted on each side Estimate and extrapolation to cutoff

What must be true

No single test establishes RD validity. Each diagnostic asks whether the data behave as the design predicts.

Requirement How to probe it Expected if it holds Warning pattern
No other discontinuous change at \(c\) Audit every rule tied to the cutoff; plot outcomes that treatment cannot yet affect Only the treatment rule changes at \(c\); placebo outcomes are smooth Another policy starts at \(c\) or a placebo outcome jumps
No precise manipulation of the running variable Plot a fine histogram; estimate a density discontinuity with rddensity The density passes smoothly through \(c\) Missing mass on one side and bunching on the other
Predetermined covariates are continuous Apply the same RD plot and estimator to each baseline covariate Covariate limits meet at \(c\); isolated insignificant estimates occur at their nominal rate Economically meaningful or systematic covariate jumps
The cutoff changes treatment in a fuzzy RD Plot \(D\) against \(X\); estimate the first-stage discontinuity with rdrobust D X A clear, precisely estimated jump in treatment probability A tiny or statistically indistinguishable first stage
Fuzzy-RD exclusion and monotonicity hold Trace every pathway from eligibility to \(Y\) and every behavioral response Eligibility affects \(Y\) only through \(D\) and does not make anyone less likely to take treatment A direct eligibility effect, bundled treatment, or plausible defiers

The first three checks look for no jump. The fuzzy first stage looks for a jump. Exclusion and monotonicity require institutional evidence because they are not fully testable.

Diagnostics can reveal problems; they cannot prove the identifying assumption. Multiple covariate tests also create false positives by chance, so judge their joint pattern and economic size rather than one \(p\)-value.

Sorting changes who is compared

Smooth near the cutoff Bunching near the cutoff
Counts change gradually through zero. Units just below and above plausibly come from the same underlying population. Missing observations just below and excess observations just above suggest that some units crossed strategically. The two sides may now contain different types of units.

The density plot counts observations in narrow intervals of the running variable. We are not looking for a flat distribution; we are looking for a distribution that does not jump at the cutoff.

A density jump does not mechanically prove outcome bias. It raises the substantive question: who moved across the threshold, and could that movement change potential outcomes?

A compact workflow

  1. State the rule. Name \(R\), \(c\), \(Z\), \(D\), and \(Y\) in words.
  2. Audit the institution. Could people manipulate \(R\)? What else changes at \(c\)?
  3. Plot treatment and outcome. Show cutoff, binned means, and separate local fits.
  4. Estimate locally. Prefer local linear fits with robust bias-corrected inference.
  5. Check credibility. Inspect density, predetermined covariates, and placebo cutoffs.
  6. Show sensitivity. Report reasonable bandwidth and specification changes.
  7. Interpret locally. Say whose effect is identified.

Report the cutoff, polynomial order, kernel, selected bandwidths on both sides, effective sample sizes, estimate, standard error or confidence interval, and whether inference is conventional or robust bias-corrected.

Common wrong turns

Wrong turn Why it fails Better practice
Compare everyone above with everyone below Distant units may be very different Estimate locally near the cutoff
Call “above cutoff” the treatment group in a fuzzy RD Eligibility and receipt differ Keep \(Z\) and \(D\) separate
Use one regression line through both sides Forces the jump to zero Fit separate intercepts and slopes
Use a high-order global polynomial Edge behavior can drive the estimate Use local linear or quadratic fits
Read a slope as the RD effect RD concerns the jump Compare right and left limits at \(c\)
Treat balance tests as proof Failure to reject is not validation Combine plots, institutional facts, and diagnostics
Generalize to the full population The design identifies a local effect Describe the margin and justify any extrapolation

Check your understanding

A lender automatically recommends approval when a small-business applicant’s internal credit score is at least 70. Loan receipt rises from 35% just below 70 to 75% just above 70 because loan officers can override the recommendation. One-year capital expenditure rises by $8,000 at the same cutoff.

  1. What are the running variable, cutoff, eligibility rule, treatment, and outcome?
  2. Is the design sharp or fuzzy?
  3. What is the first-stage jump, using above-cutoff minus below-cutoff?
  4. What is the fuzzy RD estimate?
  5. For whom is that estimate informative?
NoteAnswer

The running variable is the internal credit score, the cutoff is 70, assignment is the automatic approval recommendation, treatment is loan receipt, and the outcome is one-year capital expenditure. The design is fuzzy. The first stage is \(0.75-0.35=0.40\). The fuzzy RD estimate is \(\$8{,}000/0.40=\$20{,}000\) of capital expenditure for applicants near 70 whose loan receipt is changed by the automatic recommendation.
